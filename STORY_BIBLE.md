# THE 13TH NIGHT
## Comprehensive Story Bible & 13-Night Exploration Plan

**Genre:** First-person psychological survival horror  
**Primary location:** Blackwater Sanatorium  
**Target format:** One continuous browser horror game, playable on desktop and Android  
**Pacing target:** A minimum of 20 minutes of meaningful active play per night; approximately 25–30 minutes for players who investigate optional evidence  
**Story rule:** The game does not confirm what death means. Every ending is a possible experience, not an authoritative explanation of the afterlife.

---

## 1. The problem this version solves

In the current build, Night 1 can move on as soon as the player reads the power procedure, collects the keycard, and crosses the intake gate. That makes a major chapter transition depend on three quick interactions. The player has not yet learned the building, investigated its history, or had time to become suspicious of what they are seeing.

The new story is built around a different rule:

> **A night ends because the player has completed an investigation and faced that night's turning point—not because they crossed a threshold.**

Every night has five parts:

1. **Routine:** Give the player a practical reason to explore and make the building initially legible.
2. **Investigation:** Require visits to several areas, inspection of equipment, and comparison of clues.
3. **Complication:** Something makes the original explanation insufficient. It should be discovered, not announced.
4. **Confrontation:** A puzzle, stealth sequence, environmental change, or dangerous return trip makes the discovery playable.
5. **Resolution:** The player returns to a meaningful location, files or interprets the evidence, and sees the next chapter begin.

There is no countdown that simply waits for twenty minutes to pass. The minimum duration comes from useful actions, navigation, puzzle-solving, and the need to revisit locations with new information. Optional evidence adds further context without turning the main story into a scavenger hunt for its own sake.

## 2. The premise

The player is an unnamed night-security guard at Blackwater Sanatorium, a medical facility that was closed years ago. The assignment appears simple: check the building, keep unauthorized people out, monitor the registered camera feeds, and record electrical faults while the last records are prepared for removal.

The opening handover report is signed **M. Voss**. It describes an east wing that should remain locked, twelve registered camera feeds, a generator that is supposedly working, and a rule that sounds almost embarrassingly superstitious: if Camera 13 appears, do not look for its source. Record the fault and return to Reception.

The building is quiet enough for the guard to believe that its problems are ordinary. A keycard has been left beside the monitor. The maintenance key is supposed to be at the Nurse Station. The circuit breakers have labels. The cameras have room names. The old documents have dates and signatures.

But details refuse to agree. A roster lists twelve staff and a thirteenth blank entry. A disconnected terminal records a recent access event. A recording shows a person in a room before that person enters it. The corridor is longer on the return journey. A monitor continues showing an image after its power has been removed.

For the first six nights, the guard explains these things away as faults, vandalism, exhausted perception, or a problem with the building's records. From Night 7 onward, the sanatorium stops behaving like a place and begins behaving like a memory: the same rooms recur, but their arrangement and meaning change. The Watcher appears more often. It does not behave like a conventional monster that wants to kill the player. It waits for the guard to decide what it is.

The last documents establish the truth without explaining every supernatural detail: **the guard was found dead in the security booth at 01:13 AM.** The booth door was unlocked from the inside. The monitor was disconnected but still showing a live corridor. There was no intruder in the building. The incident report's cause-of-death field is blank.

The player has been exploring the final hours of a life, reconstructed by a mind that cannot let go of its last fear. Whether the sanatorium is an afterlife, a dying consciousness, a memory, or something that cannot be named is never objectively settled.

## 3. What really happened (author-facing truth, not all revealed to the player)

On the guard's final shift at Blackwater, the old emergency power system failed. Just before 01:13 AM, the security monitor displayed a thirteenth camera. There was no Camera 13 in the building's official system. The feed showed a corridor and a figure facing the lens. It might have been a person, a distorted reflection, or a bad image.

The guard heard knocking. He could not determine whether it came from the booth door, the corridor, a pipe in the walls, or the radio. He had a profound fear of opening a door when he could not see what waited beyond it. He believed that as long as he watched the cameras and kept every door under control, the unknown could not reach him.

He remained at the console rather than opening the door. A power fault caused smoke and electrical arcing in or near the booth. The guard lost consciousness and died at approximately 01:13 AM. The precise medical cause is never filled in on the report. The record does not establish whether anyone was actually outside the booth calling for help.

His final fear was not simply “a monster is behind the door.” It was the fear of **being unable to know, control, or verify what was happening—and being alone when control failed**. He had mistaken constant watching for safety. He spent his last moments looking at the image rather than facing the darkness beyond the screen.

The thirteen nights are not necessarily thirteen literal dates. They are thirteen layers of the guard's final shift, arranged by a mind that understands experience through night-duty reports. To the player, they initially look like consecutive shifts. Later, the clock's refusal to move and the repeating spaces make that interpretation impossible to maintain. The game never supplies a scientific diagram of this process.

### The central emotional turn

The story must not end with the guard defeating an evil creature. It ends when he can finally admit that he is afraid, stop demanding proof of what is behind the door, and choose whether to open it anyway. The door is not proven safe. The darkness is not proven empty. He acts without certainty.

## 4. Important characters and voices

### The Guard — the player character

Never name him in the main game. His name is erased, cropped, or hidden by the angle of a badge. The absence of his name should feel institutional at first and personal later. Do not explain his personality in a biography. Let it emerge through choices, repeated phrases, how he documents small details, and his growing discomfort with his own handwriting.

He is observant, methodical, and frightened of mistakes. At first, he turns fear into procedure: check the door, check the camera, write a report. His private thoughts should be short, specific, and restrained. Avoid constant quips or lengthy narration during exploration.

### M. Voss — the handover name

M. Voss is listed as the author of the opening report. The player should never meet Voss in a normal, clear conversation. The name appears on documents; a voice might briefly sound like it in a damaged recording, but the sound cannot be confidently identified. Voss represents the ordinary explanation the guard wants: there was a previous worker, there are procedures, and somebody knows how the building works.

### Dispatch — the radio voice

Calm, procedural, and increasingly difficult to locate. It begins by responding like a tired dispatcher. Later, it appears to know where the guard is without using the camera system. It occasionally says lines the guard has not yet spoken. Do not make Dispatch openly threatening. Its most frightening moments should sound like an exhausted person trying to get the guard to stop doing something that no longer helps.

### The Watcher

A tall, indistinct figure in the same posture as the guard: shoulders held tight, head turned toward a screen, hands near an invisible console. It should not roar, attack on sight, or behave like a conventional stalking monster in every chapter. Early sightings are quick. Later it waits in a room the player has already searched.

The reveal is not “the Watcher is an evil double.” It is the possibility that the guard has been looking at a version of himself who remained at the console. Whether the figure is a supernatural presence or a visual form of guilt and fear remains uncertain.

## 5. Design rules for the whole game

### Minimum playtime

The time targets below describe **active play**, not a stopwatch. Mandatory content in each night should take at least 20 minutes for a reasonably observant first-time player. Optional clue routes add approximately 5–10 minutes. Do not use long empty walks, repeated unskippable waits, or walls of text to inflate time.

### The building

Use the rooms and objects already established by the build wherever possible:

- Reception and the security monitor
- Main Hall and intake gate
- Records Office
- Nurse Station
- Ward 13
- Generator Room
- Dormitory Annex
- Hydrotherapy Wing
- Isolation Ward
- Chapel
- Service Connector
- Basement Archive
- Isolation Passage
- Quiet Room

Not every space needs to be newly modelled for every chapter. Recontextualize existing spaces with light states, access restrictions, furniture visibility, sign changes, CCTV images, document placement, and sound. After Night 6, the building can change in ways that are visually simple but narratively meaningful: an extra doorway, a repeated sign, a room visible on a screen but not in front of the player, or a corridor that appears longer after the player turns around.

### Threat pacing

- **Nights 1–2:** No sustained chase. Threat is mostly implication, shadows, and equipment behaving incorrectly.
- **Nights 3–4:** The player briefly sees evidence that a figure may be present. The first real stalking sequence happens only after the player has reason to fear it.
- **Night 5:** First meaningful pursuit or hiding sequence; teach the player the danger rules.
- **Night 6:** Threat becomes psychologically personal rather than more physically aggressive.
- **Nights 7–12:** The Watcher appears in increasingly deliberate ways. It is not necessary to chase the player every night. Sometimes it is more frightening when it simply remains in view.
- **Night 13:** No ordinary monster chase. The final challenge is the guard's refusal to act without proof.

### Evidence must change interpretation

A document should not exist only to say “this place is haunted.” Each one should add a new contradiction, connect two previously separate details, or change the meaning of an earlier scene. A player who reads carefully should begin predicting the reveal just before it becomes explicit.

### Main objectives vs. optional evidence

Every night has several mandatory stage flags. A single note, key, or door must not complete a night. Optional clues provide extra emotional texture and may alter final narration or the afterlife choice's phrasing, but they should not hide the basic story behind an obscure collectible.

# 6. The thirteen nights

## NIGHT 01 — THE FIRST BLACKOUT
**Target:** 22–25 minutes mandatory; 27–32 minutes with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Teach the player the building's ordinary logic before breaking it. Build tension without making the player feel hunted immediately.

### Opening

The guard wakes slumped in the reception chair. The ceiling light is on, but it takes a moment to stop flickering. The wall clock shows **01:13**. His first thought is that he must have dozed off. He reads the two-page handover report and finds the line about Camera 13. It looks like a joke written by a tired employee.

The intake gate is not an exit. It separates Reception from the staff areas that the guard has been assigned to inspect. The player should discover that opening it begins the shift's investigation, not ends it.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Handover and equipment check (4–5 min)**

- Read both pages of the handover report.
- Check the wall clock, security monitor, desk radio, flashlight, and room map.
- Inspect the keycard and the reception log separately. The log mentions a previous blackout at 01:13 even though the current handover insists the generator is normal.
- Use the keycard to open the intake gate. The first gate opening is a small achievement, not the night's finish.

**Stage B — Initial rounds (5–6 min)**

- Walk the Main Hall and test the registered camera labels against their physical room signs.
- Inspect the Records Office and confirm that the east-wing closure notice is newer than the documents it covers.
- Check the Nurse Station for the maintenance key's labelled hook. The hook is empty, but a handwritten inventory claims the key is present.
- Check the Dormitory Annex roster board. Several staff entries have end times; the line for Night Security ends at 01:13.

**Stage C — Compare the paperwork (4–5 min)**

- Return to Records Office with the camera register in mind. Find the restricted access register: twelve authorized staff, twelve cameras, twelve issued keys, and a thirteenth line that only says NIGHT SECURITY.
- Compare the register with the opening report. The issue is not just that a camera is missing: the security position itself looks as though it was added after the official records were created.
- Find a maintenance note that says the east wing has been closed for years. The door's paint is chipped from the inside, as if it has been opened recently.

**Stage D — Follow the fault (4–5 min)**

- A registered feed freezes. The player must visit at least two camera locations to verify whether the problem is local or limited to the monitor.
- At the second location, the room is empty, but the monitor shows a silhouette in the room for one frame.
- The lights briefly go out. The player hears three knocks, but when they reach the apparent source, there is nothing to interact with. The game should not identify the source.

**Stage E — File the report (3–4 min)**

- Return to Reception and use the desk log to record the checklist: intake secured, ward signs checked, register discrepancy noted, camera fault unresolved.
- The screen asks whether to classify the fault as **EQUIPMENT**, **UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY**, or **UNCONFIRMED**. All are plausible; none has an immediate obvious reward.
- After filing, the CCTV monitor switches to a hallway angle that the player has not seen before. The timecode changes from 01:12 to 01:13. The feed returns to normal before the guard can walk there.

### Clue and text beats

- **Reception Log:** Retain the existing note about the desk clock stopping at 01:13 and the monitor glass reflecting someone behind the guard.
- **Closed-Wing Access Register:** Retain the thirteenth entry labelled NIGHT SECURITY and the disconnected terminal detail.
- **Optional: Torn Maintenance Receipt:** “The camera system was disconnected from this wing before the last inventory. Someone has continued to submit faults from it.”
- **Inner thought after seeing the silhouette:** “The room was empty when I got there. I know because I checked the room before I checked the screen.”

### End-of-night event

The player must finish all four rounds and submit the report. The intake gate remains open, but the building's fluorescent lights reset in a sequence that runs away from the player. The clock is still 01:13. A small night-card transition marks the next record. No chase occurs.

**Progression rule:** The night may not complete on keycard collection or crossing the intake gate. It requires handover read, all required inspection stations visited, register discrepancy recorded, and final report submitted.

## NIGHT 02 — MANUAL OVERRIDE
**Target:** 22–26 minutes mandatory; 27–32 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Turn the player from a visitor into a problem-solver. Introduce a functional puzzle and show that fixing the building can make it less trustworthy.

### Premise

The night record begins with the same reception desk, but the monitor is now dark and the maintenance key is missing from the location where the checklist says it should be. The guard interprets this as a normal equipment failure and follows the established procedure.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Trace the outage (4 min)**

- Read the new maintenance ticket at Reception.
- Check three registered camera feeds. One has no picture; one is stuck on the empty Main Hall; one shows the Generator Room's status lamp glowing although the room itself is dark.
- Visit the Nurse Station and locate the maintenance key. It is tucked behind a clipboard rather than sitting in plain view.

**Stage B — Learn the circuit (4–5 min)**

- Find the maintenance memo before touching the breaker panel.
- Match the three colours to their functions: AMBER for emergency lighting, BLUE for ward locks, RED for the lift.
- The player discovers a warning: activating all circuits at once can make the cameras start recording before the lights return.

**Stage C — Reach and restore the Generator Room (5–6 min)**

- Unlock the Generator Room and inspect three panels, not just one console.
- Find a blown fuse, a manual bypass, and a handwritten log describing a previous outage at 01:13.
- Restore the circuit in the specified **AMBER → BLUE → RED** sequence. The puzzle should require the player to visit or verify the relevant panels, not simply press three prompts in order from one spot.

**Stage D — Verify the repair (4–5 min)**

- Return by a route that is initially darker than the route used to enter.
- Check the monitor and confirm that the lights come back in the wrong order: the Ward 13 camera returns first, then Reception, then the Main Hall.
- Visit the ward entrance and inspect its lock indicator. The lock opens normally; behind the door, no one is visible.

**Stage E — Complete the shift ticket (4–5 min)**

- Return to Reception and record the voltage status.
- The monitor shows a figure standing behind the guard's chair. Turning around reveals no one.
- Before the next night begins, the Ward 13 feed briefly shows twelve occupied beds. When the image stabilizes, the room is empty.

### Clue and text beats

- **Maintenance Note:** Keep the existing warning about cameras recording before the lights come back.
- **Generator Log:** Keep the detail that the image remains after the camera feed and monitor have been disconnected.
- **Optional: Fuse Box Scratch Marks:** The scratches look like someone counted repeated attempts to switch the circuits off.
- **Radio line:** “Maintenance, please confirm the room is empty before you restore the ward feed.” A pause. “You already confirmed it.”

### End-of-night event

The power stays on, but the camera feed does not show a fully empty ward anymore. A bed sheet shifts with no visible body beneath it. The player completes the maintenance ticket and seals the generator panel. Only then does the shift record advance.

**Progression rule:** The night requires the key, the memo, successful circuit repair, a post-repair camera check, and a submitted ticket. Power restoration alone does not finish it.

## NIGHT 03 — THE WARD WITH TWELVE BEDS
**Target:** 22–26 minutes mandatory; 28–33 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Make Ward 13 the first major investigative set piece. Introduce the idea that the records contain a missing person who is not simply another patient.

### Premise

Power has returned. The guard's new assignment is to verify an irregular ward index because the system reports an extra bed card. The ward has twelve bed frames. The chart contains thirteen slips. The player should have time to examine the room and understand that this is not a random scare.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Establish the ward layout (4–5 min)**

- Enter Ward 13 and inspect the entrance, bedside lamps, and the twelve bed frames.
- Check bed numbers in order. At least three should contradict the index in a different way: a number has been swapped, a label has been freshly scraped, or the chart references a bed that does not physically exist.
- The room should initially be quiet; no monster reveal during the first pass.

**Stage B — Recover three index slips (6–7 min)**

- Find one slip in the Records Office file stack, one among the bed charts in Ward 13, and one underneath the last bed.
- The slips reveal three independent discrepancies: the staff member's name is erased; the number of beds does not match the index; and corridor measurements increase across documents dated on the same night.
- Each slip gives part of the archive seal code or a symbol that can be used to infer it. No slip should contain the full solution on its own.

**Stage C — Investigate the child drawing (3–4 min)**

- The optional drawing beneath the final bed shows the corridor as a long rectangle and a faceless figure at its end.
- The back says: “WHEN YOU LOOK AT HIM, HE IS NOT THERE. WHEN YOU LOOK AWAY, HE IS WAITING.”
- The drawing should be unsettling because it describes an observation rule, not because it depicts a gore scene.

**Stage D — Solve the archive seal (4–5 min)**

- Return to the Records Office and compare the slips with the access register.
- Use the three slips to derive the seal sequence. If the player guesses incorrectly, the panel does not trigger a jumpscare; the wrong sequence makes a distant ward light turn on and stay on.
- Once solved, the Basement Archive door unlocks, but the player still needs to inspect the ward's final bedside chart and confirm the discrepancy.

**Stage E — Finish the ward audit (4–5 min)**

- Find the final chart stating **STATUS: NOT DISCHARGED** and **BED NO.: 7** with the patient identity removed.
- A fluorescent light clicks on over the bed with no number. The player hears fabric shifting, but there is no visible body.
- Return to the ward entrance and file the audit before descending toward the archive.

### Clue and text beats

- **Damaged Personnel Slip:** “Do not count the person watching the cameras. He is already included.”
- **Patient Index Slip:** “The corridor was measured at 28 metres, then 34, then 51. I walked back to the desk. It took longer on the return.”
- **Final Index Slip:** “The last recording is not from a camera. It is from the person watching.”
- **Inner thought:** “Twelve beds. Thirteen records. I keep trying to find the missing person by counting everything except myself.”

### End-of-night event

After the audit is filed, the player hears twelve soft bed-frame creaks, followed by one from the empty thirteenth position. The room's lights turn off from the far end toward the door. The player must walk back to the main hall under emergency lights. The chapter transitions only after the archive access has been earned and the ward audit completed.

**Progression rule:** Three slips, archive seal solution, and completed ward audit are mandatory. Do not auto-complete immediately when the third slip is collected.

## NIGHT 04 — THE ORIGINAL RECORDINGS
**Target:** 23–27 minutes mandatory; 29–34 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Use CCTV and the Basement Archive to turn scattered oddities into evidence. The first major threat should arise only after the player has invested in the investigation.

### Premise

The archive seal is open. Three surveillance reels were stored after an operator reported a figure on an unregistered feed. The official transfer memo says the reels were taken from the archive years apart, yet all three have the same signature.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Enter and catalogue the archive (4 min)**

- Check the archive sign, shelf numbering, and transfer memo.
- The shelf has three empty spaces labelled A, B, and C, plus one extra space with no label.
- The old index says the reels were removed, but there are fresh dust outlines showing that the reels were returned recently.

**Stage B — Recover three reels (6–7 min)**

- Reel A is behind a locked cabinet whose key is in a nearby records tray.
- Reel B is accessible only after the player restores power to the archive playback deck.
- Reel C is in a drawer with a label that has been scratched away. Its audio contains a pause longer than the physical reel could hold.
- Each retrieval triggers one subtle change in sound or lighting. The first reel only causes a chair creak; the second causes the sound to appear behind the player; the third makes the light remain off for a beat longer than expected.

**Stage C — Match the timecodes (4–5 min)**

- Use the console to compare the three recordings. All show the same corridor from different angles.
- None shows the figure entering the hallway. It appears after the clock changes from 01:12 to 01:13.
- One reel appears to show the guard in a different room at the same moment as the other reels show him at Reception.

**Stage D — Verify one image in the building (4–5 min)**

- The player must return to the corresponding corridor location and inspect it directly.
- The corridor is empty, but the player discovers a scuff mark or chair impression that matches the recorded image.
- A low sound begins behind the archive shelves. The player must use the room's layout to exit without turning the return trip into a long chase.

**Stage E — Return and close the evidence report (4–5 min)**

- File the three reel IDs in the correct order: A, B, C, based on the timecodes rather than the shelf labels.
- The sound of the security-booth chair appears in all three reels, though no chair is shown in the recorded corridor.
- The Watcher is first glimpsed at a distance after the report is saved. It vanishes when the player turns directly toward it.

### Clue and text beats

- **Archive Transfer Memo:** Retain the fact that an approving signature appears on documents dated twelve years apart.
- **Reel A — The Operator:** “If I turn around, there will be nothing there. If I keep looking at the screen, I can pretend someone is coming to help.”
- **Reel B — The Watcher:** “You keep asking what is outside the door. You never ask why you are still inside.”
- **Reel C — 01:13:** “We found you in the booth.” The guard answers, “Then who's answering?” The recording ends with a light switch clicking.

### End-of-night event

The archive console displays **SIGNAL MATCHED** even though the player has not touched the final input. One additional audio channel is present on the playback interface, labelled only **LOCAL**. The interface then shuts down. The chapter does not end until all three reels have been recovered, synchronized, and the evidence report filed.

**Progression rule:** Evidence count alone is insufficient. Require all three reels, a successful synchronization step, and a return to the archive console.

## NIGHT 05 — THE UNCLAIMED DOOR
**Target:** 24–28 minutes mandatory; 30–36 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Turn investigation into danger. The player learns to use hiding and route knowledge, then encounters the first significant pursuit. The encounter should feel earned by the evidence, not random.

### Premise

The recordings are synchronized, but the console cannot explain the extra audio channel. A brass key referenced in an old maintenance ledger is stored in the Chapel. The key is required to open the Quiet Room, a room absent from the official floor plan.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Decode the signal (4–5 min)**

- Revisit the archive console and line up three sound peaks with the three 01:13 timestamps.
- The signal is not a spoken password. It is a sequence of pauses and knocks that can be matched to the recording order.
- If the player makes an error, the console plays a quiet fragment of the guard's breathing rather than resetting with an obvious failure message.

**Stage B — Search the Chapel (4–5 min)**

- Follow the transfer memo to the Chapel and inspect three locations: the prayer rack, an empty candle stand, and a locked donation drawer.
- The brass key is inside the drawer, but the drawer's label says it was never installed.
- Read the unsigned prayer card about not calling a person back after they have already gone.

**Stage C — A route that no longer agrees (4 min)**

- Return toward the Quiet Room. One familiar door takes the player back to a room they have already crossed, but the furniture orientation is reversed.
- The minimap should not simply show a new route as an answer; the player has to use room signage, the key, and the archive notes to pick the correct route.
- A first short encounter with the Watcher occurs beyond the player's direct line of sight. The player hears movement and sees the figure at the far end of the hall only after checking a nearby room.

**Stage D — First survival sequence (5–6 min)**

- The player must cross one exposed section while the Watcher changes position when not observed.
- Use the established hide mechanic in a supply alcove or behind the nurse-station furniture. The hiding period should be short enough that the player remains involved; don't make them wait helplessly for long stretches.
- The Watcher does not rush in and attack as soon as the player hides. It stands in the corridor, listens, then moves away only after the player stops moving and turns off the flashlight.

**Stage E — Unlock the Quiet Room and verify the record (4–5 min)**

- Use the brass key on the Quiet Room door.
- Before entering, inspect the door plate. Its screws are newer than the door; there is no room number underneath the fresh paint.
- Enter, inspect the chair arrangement and the register, but do not yet trigger Night 6's final entry. The player should leave knowing the room seems prepared for a person who has not arrived.

### Clue and text beats

- **Unsigned Prayer Card:** “Fear can keep a room locked long after the key has turned.”
- **Optional Chapel Photograph:** One member of the old security staff has been scratched out. Only the sleeve and brass badge remain. On the back: “He was not cruel. He was frightened. The last thing he asked for was more light.”
- **Radio:** “Security, do not approach the sound until you can identify it.” Static. “You don't have to identify it.”

### End-of-night event

The player exits the Quiet Room and hears three knocks from inside it. When they look back, the door is still closed. It is now possible to see a faint light underneath it, though the room has no power circuit listed in the panel. The night ends after the key's chain of custody is recorded and the first encounter is survived.

**Progression rule:** Signal synchronized, key obtained, survival sequence completed, and Quiet Room inspected. Picking up the brass key must not instantly advance the chapter.

## NIGHT 06 — THE QUIET ROOM
**Target:** 22–27 minutes mandatory; 28–34 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Complete the first half's grounded mystery. The player still thinks a real building is being investigated, but evidence begins to frame the story around the guard himself.

### Premise

The Quiet Room's register lists six entries completed and a seventh written in ink that seems to have soaked through from the next page. The guard is asked to complete a final inventory because the room is not on the official floor plan.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Inspect the Quiet Room properly (4 min)**

- Inspect all chairs, the empty wall clock, the register, and the door from the inside.
- The chairs face the door. Each has a faint indentation as if someone sat there, except one chair with a fresh indentation despite no dust being disturbed around it.
- The register contains the words **THE BUILDING WILL STOP PRETENDING TO BE A BUILDING NOW**. The guard should not immediately interpret it as a supernatural confession.

**Stage B — Recover four memory fragments (7–8 min)**

- **Hydrotherapy Wing:** a reflection shows the guard standing behind himself.
- **Isolation Ward:** a radio transcript captures three knocks at the security booth door and a second breath exactly in time with the guard's own.
- **Chapel:** a photo fragment describes a frightened security worker asking for more light.
- **Dormitory Annex:** a staff roster lists Security's shift end as 01:13 AM and says the shift ends when the person on duty stops waiting for the lights.
- The player must physically visit the locations and compare the fragments in the field notes. The fragments are not four interchangeable pickups: each adds a different aspect of the final event.

**Stage C — Test the explanations (4–5 min)**

- At Reception, compare the radio transcript, the roster, and the generator log.
- Try the normal explanations: faulty radio, mirror distortion, bad camera clock, and a misfiled staff record.
- The game should permit the guard to write these as possible explanations, but each one fails against at least one independent clue.

**Stage D — Re-enter the Quiet Room (4–5 min)**

- The four fragments make a line in the register visible under a certain angle of light: “If you want the shift to end, stop treating every sound as a warning.”
- There is no monster attack in this sequence. The player hears distant ambience stop, then hears a single ordinary, human breath from somewhere nearby.
- The player must choose to sit in the empty chair or stand facing the door for a short, reflective interaction. Neither choice gives a reward badge; it simply changes the first inner-thought line in Night 7.

**Stage E — Complete the six-night record (3–4 min)**

- File the six findings in the register. The page turns without being touched.
- The next line has a time rather than a task: **01:13 — DO NOT LOOK AT THE MONITOR TO CHECK WHETHER YOU ARE STILL HERE.**
- The fluorescent lights do not all go out. Instead, ordinary room tone gradually disappears, leaving the sound of a pen rolling on a desk.

### Clue and text beats

Keep the existing memory fragments as the foundation. Add one short note in the Quiet Room:

> “Six entries verified. One person remained at the post. The next record is not a record of the building.”

### End-of-night event

The player returns to Reception and sees the map redraw one corridor slightly longer than before. No one is shown moving furniture. Night 7 begins with the same physical map but one impossible discrepancy that cannot be dismissed as a power failure.

**Progression rule:** All four memory fragments, explanation check, Quiet Room register interaction, and final Reception report must be completed. Night 6 is the end of the grounded investigation arc; Night 7 begins reality-breaking, not earlier.

## NIGHT 07 — THE CORRIDOR MEASURES MORE
**Target:** 22–27 minutes mandatory; 28–35 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Change the rules of space. The player should understand that the floor plan itself is becoming part of the mystery.

### Premise

The building appears almost unchanged. The map still records a corridor of 28.4 metres from the intake gate to the Service Connector. A new measurement sheet says it is now 41.7 metres. The sheet is signed in the guard's handwriting.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Establish the baseline (4 min)**

- Read the Night 1 floor plan and the new measurement sheet side by side.
- Walk the Main Hall and count ceiling lamps, door signs, and floor seams.
- The player should be able to form a clear expectation of the route before the anomaly appears.

**Stage B — Measure three sections (6–7 min)**

- Measure the intake-to-records route, the ward-to-connector route, and the connector-to-archive route using fixed markings or interactable wall plaques.
- Each returns a different result on the way back than on the way out.
- The player must decide whether to mark the measured route or trust the old map. Each decision opens a different route but neither removes the contradiction.

**Stage C — Revisit familiar rooms (4–5 min)**

- The Records Office is in the expected position, but its door opens toward the wrong wall. The furniture still fits the room, yet the door is not where the map says it is.
- A hallway sign repeats the words **SECURITY BOOTH** in two directions.
- One familiar room appears beyond its own doorway for a moment before returning to normal.

**Stage D — Reach the measurement endpoint (4–5 min)**

- The player follows a series of five light fixtures. The sheet says there are five; a sixth is visible in the distance.
- The sixth light is not a normal collectible marker. The player must approach it and inspect the wall seam beneath it.
- The seam has the same chipped paint as the wall behind the reception chair.

**Stage E — Return without the map (4–5 min)**

- The map display begins updating when the player looks away from it. The player has to navigate back using door signs and visual memory for one short segment.
- At Reception, the recorded route is now 41.7 metres on both the sheet and the map, though no walls were moved while the player was in the room.

### Clue and text beats

- **Corridor Measurement Sheet:** Retain the 28.4m-to-41.7m contradiction and the note that the corridor moved when the writer looked at the markings.
- **Optional: Old Measurement Tape:** A section of the tape is worn smooth where a measurement has been crossed out repeatedly. The last written value is 0.0m, as though the tape were measured from the guard's chair.
- **Inner thought:** “I was measuring the building. Somewhere along the way, I started measuring how far away I was from the desk.”

### End-of-night event

The guard returns to Reception. The entrance is in the correct place, but the monitor shows a longer corridor behind the chair. The Watcher stands at the far end of that image. It does not appear in the actual room.

**Progression rule:** Require three measurement comparisons, discovery of the sixth light, and the return trip without relying on the map. Reading the new sheet alone cannot advance the night.

## NIGHT 08 — CAMERA 13
**Target:** 22–27 minutes mandatory; 28–34 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Make the player doubt the camera system—the tool that has represented safety, verification, and control throughout the game.

### Premise

A new feed has appeared in the CCTV menu. It is labelled **CAM 13**. It shows a security booth from an angle that should be impossible because no camera is installed inside the monitor. The feed includes the guard sitting in the chair.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Test the feed (4–5 min)**

- Cycle through the twelve registered feeds and verify that each is normal or predictably faulty.
- Select Camera 13. The image has a live timecode of 01:13, but its compression, noise, and room layout differ from the actual Reception.
- The player must leave the console and inspect Reception directly.

**Stage B — Cross-check three shown details (5–6 min)**

- The feed shows a chair facing the wrong direction, a lamp that is absent from the room, and an open door behind the chair.
- The player visits the relevant locations and identifies which details are impossible, which are simply hidden from the current angle, and which appear only after looking away.
- The goal is not to prove Camera 13 is a normal device. It is to show that the feed changes based on the player's attention.

**Stage C — Follow a moving timecode (4–5 min)**

- The timecode shifts from 01:12:54 to 01:13:00 and then refuses to advance.
- The player checks the wall clock, the radio clock, and the camera log. All have different seconds but the same minute.
- The feed briefly displays the Watcher's shoulders and hands. The figure's posture matches the guard's first-person camera height.

**Stage D — Camera blind spot route (4–5 min)**

- To complete the test, the player must cross two rooms without using CCTV or the map as a guide, then return to the monitor and check whether Camera 13 captured the crossing.
- The feed shows the player still seated in Reception throughout the entire test.
- The audio track has footsteps arriving at the console, even though the recorded figure never stands up.

**Stage E — File the unregistered feed report (4–5 min)**

- Choose whether the report should classify Camera 13 as **UNKNOWN HARDWARE**, **SIGNAL LOOP**, or **UNVERIFIED IMAGE**.
- The options should influence the guard's wording in his notes but not produce a “correct” answer.
- A final still frame turns to face the viewer before the feed goes black.

### Clue and text beats

- **Camera 13 Frame:** Keep the existing line that the feed has no camera number and is labelled LIVE.
- **Optional: Maintenance Inventory:** “The system cannot receive an image from equipment that does not exist. Do not write that sentence in the official report.”
- **Inner thought:** “If the camera shows me sitting here, who has been holding the flashlight?”

### End-of-night event

The console asks the player to confirm the operator's identity. The name field contains a blinking cursor but no text. The player must close the report manually. The screen goes black and reflects the guard in the chair for one brief moment.

**Progression rule:** Camera 13 must be tested, its three visual details cross-checked, the blind-spot route completed, and a report filed. Merely opening the feed is not enough.

## NIGHT 09 — THE VOICE ON THE RADIO
**Target:** 23–28 minutes mandatory; 29–36 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Make the story intimate. The first voice that seems to know the guard is not the Watcher but his own radio, which has always sounded ordinary.

### Premise

The personal radio receives a message from Dispatch. The message begins with a routine instruction and ends with the calm assertion that the guard is still sitting in the chair. The guard is standing in the Dormitory Annex when he hears it.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Recover three transcript fragments (6–7 min)**

- Find the first fragment in the Dormitory Annex, the second near Hydrotherapy, and the third in the Isolation Ward.
- The fragments are not printed in playback order. The player must use waveform marks, time stamps, and a shared background sound to order them.
- The same three knocks occur at different times on each fragment, but the recording's perspective changes.

**Stage B — Reconstruct the exchange (4–5 min)**

- Assemble the fragments at the Reception radio.
- The message reads:
  - DISPATCH: “You can stand up now.”
  - GUARD: “I am standing.”
  - DISPATCH: “You're still in the chair.”
  - GUARD: “I can hear you behind the door.”
  - DISPATCH: “There is no door between us.”
- The final line is in the guard's handwriting even though he does not remember writing it.

**Stage C — Locate the source (4–5 min)**

- Test the radio in three places: Reception, Hydrotherapy, and the Chapel.
- The signal grows strongest in one location but the handset's direction indicator points back to Reception.
- The player should be able to return to the source without being told “go to X.” Follow static changes, not a giant objective marker.

**Stage D — Radio blackout traversal (4–5 min)**

- Move through a portion of the building with the radio switched off. Ambient sound is reduced, and the player must use room labels and memory.
- When the radio is turned back on, it plays the guard's own footsteps several seconds before he makes them.
- The Watcher may appear at a distance, but this is a listening sequence, not a chase.

**Stage E — Complete the voice report (4–5 min)**

- Return to Reception and record the reconstructed conversation.
- The player must select one of three descriptions for the voice: **DISPATCH**, **MY OWN VOICE**, or **SOURCE UNKNOWN**. There is no universal correct choice.
- After filing, the radio clicks on by itself and says, very softly: “You don't need permission to stand up.”

### Clue and text beats

- **Radio Transcript:** Keep the existing dialogue, including the note that the sheet is warm and the final line is in the guard's handwriting.
- **Optional: Radio Battery Cover:** A pencil inscription underneath reads: “I kept turning it on because silence gave me nothing to check.”

### End-of-night event

The radio is placed on the desk by the player. The next time the player looks at it, it is already in the same position—only now there is a wet thumbprint on the speaker grille. No one is shown touching it.

**Progression rule:** Recover and order all three transcript fragments, test the radio in all three locations, complete the blackout traversal, and file a source classification.

## NIGHT 10 — THE UNBUILT WING
**Target:** 24–29 minutes mandatory; 30–37 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Take away reliable space and navigation. The player must stop expecting one correct floor plan to explain every route.

### Premise

An architect's plan contains no corridor beyond Isolation. Yet the guard can see a passage continuing past the west wall. Different old surveys disagree about whether the passage ends, turns into a ward, or continues beyond the page.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Compare three plans (4–5 min)**

- Find an old plan in the Records Office, a maintenance sketch near Isolation, and a floor plan attached to the archive transfer.
- Each map is accurate in one section and impossible in another.
- Mark the overlap between them. The overlap identifies a route through rooms already known to the player, rather than providing a new maze that must be randomly searched.

**Stage B — Test the corridor rules (5–6 min)**

- Walk the unbuilt passage once quickly, once slowly, and once while looking back at the same wall marker.
- The distance changes with avoidance: the farther the guard tries to escape the passage, the longer it becomes.
- Approaching a door calmly does not immediately solve the problem; instead, the player finds that the door is a familiar room, mislabelled.

**Stage C — Investigate three apparent exits (5–6 min)**

- One exit returns to the Records Office, one to the Chapel, and one to a version of Reception with a chair but no monitor.
- Each exit contains a different detail from previous nights. The player must compare them to decide which version is linked to the real incident report.
- Do not make the player walk three long empty corridors. Each path should contain a clue, a changed environmental detail, or a short interaction.

**Stage D — Assemble the only stable route (4–5 min)**

- Use the stable landmarks identified earlier—lamp pattern, chipped wall, and door plate—to choose a route through the changing passage.
- The result is a direct path to Isolation, but the game does not present it as a victory over the architecture. It simply holds still long enough for the player to pass.

**Stage E — Read the handwritten annotation (4–5 min)**

- Find a note pressed so hard into the blueprint that it tears through the paper: “IT GETS BIGGER WHEN I TRY TO GET AWAY.”
- The handwriting matches the measurements from Night 7.
- Back at Reception, the old floor plan has been redrawn so that every corridor appears to lead back to the security chair.

### Clue and text beats

- **Unbuilt Wing Plan:** Keep the existing contradictory surveys and the note about escape making the corridor longer.
- **Optional: Architect's Correction:** “The north wall is correct. The room is also correct. The error occurs when the observer assumes the two are separate.”

### End-of-night event

The guard marks the stable route on the plan. When he puts the pen down, the drawn line continues past the edge of the paper and circles the security booth. The player has not drawn that final section. Do not use a loud sting; use the sound of the pencil moving after the hand has stopped.

**Progression rule:** Three plans compared, three apparent exits tested, stable route assembled, and annotation read. Entering the passage alone cannot advance the night.

## NIGHT 11 — THE WATCHER
**Target:** 24–30 minutes mandatory; 30–37 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Reframe the apparent monster without deflating the horror. The Watcher becomes more visible, but less like a predator and more like a witness who has been waiting for the guard to notice something.

### Premise

The Watcher is now visible in places where it used to vanish. It does not always move when the player looks away. Its posture resembles the guard's posture from Camera 13.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Verify three sightings (5–6 min)**

- First, find the Watcher in a CCTV image.
- Second, see its shape in the Hydrotherapy basin's reflection, even when the room behind the player is empty.
- Third, find it standing just beyond the Chapel door. If approached, it does not attack; it remains in the same posture as the figure in the camera feed.
- Each sighting gives the player a different angle, but never a clear face.

**Stage B — Collect three witness statements (5–6 min)**

- An isolation report says not to chase the figure or ask it to turn around.
- A chapel note says to let the watcher be seen without being chased.
- A repaired reel contains a line from an operator: “He is not following you. He is waiting for you to notice him.”
- Compare the statements. Their shared instruction is to stop treating the figure as something that can be solved by surveillance.

**Stage C — Hold the gaze (4–5 min)**

- In a safe room, the player must keep the Watcher in view long enough for the ambient sound to change.
- The figure is not a static jumpscare; its shoulders gradually rise and fall with the same rhythm heard in the player's own breathing.
- Looking directly at it does not trigger damage. Running away causes the route to stretch or return the player to the observation point.

**Stage D — Follow, but do not chase (4–5 min)**

- The Watcher moves from the Chapel toward the Main Hall in several visible stages.
- The player can follow at a steady walk. If the player sprints, the figure disappears and the route resets to the Chapel.
- Along the route, lights and signs repeat in the same pattern found in the security booth's reflection.

**Stage E — Read the voice behind the door transcript (4–5 min)**

- At the Chapel, find the existing transcript: the voice says the guard spent his life believing that if he watched every door, nothing could surprise him.
- After reading, the player must return to the empty observation chair and remain there for a moment without opening CCTV.
- The Watcher appears on the dark monitor glass rather than in the room.

### Clue and text beats

- Keep the existing Night 11 transcript, especially: “The part of you that was waiting for someone else to open one.”
- **Optional: Back of the Scratched Photograph:** “The person he watched was the only person he could not help.”
- **Inner thought:** “It has never tried to catch me. I have been running because I don't know what to do if it stops.”

### End-of-night event

The Watcher appears one last time in a reflection. Its hands are positioned as if they are resting on the Reception desk, just where the player's hands would be. When the player turns, the chair is empty. The monitor shows **OPERATOR PRESENT** with no camera label.

**Progression rule:** Three sightings, three witness statements, gaze sequence, walk-follow sequence, and transcript must be completed. A single note is not enough.

## NIGHT 12 — THE INCIDENT REPORT
**Target:** 25–30 minutes mandatory; 31–38 with optional evidence  
**Purpose:** Bring the physical truth of the death into view while preserving uncertainty about what happened beyond it.

### Premise

The Quiet Room register now contains an incident number. A preliminary report has appeared, but several pages have been torn or filed into other parts of the building. The guard is asked to reconstruct the report before the facility closes its record.

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Find four report fragments (6–7 min)**

- **Reception:** incident header and room designation.
- **Generator Room:** power-failure note and the last maintenance timestamp.
- **Records Office:** staff name field, damaged beyond reading.
- **Quiet Room:** attached photograph of the security chair and the missing cause-of-death page.
- A fifth optional fragment can be found in the Dormitory Annex, showing a roster with the Security end time at 01:13.

**Stage B — Reconstruct the timeline (4–5 min)**

- Sort the report fragments into this order: power failure, unregistered image, repeated knocking, no radio response, guard found unresponsive.
- The player should notice that the sequence never says anyone entered the booth.
- Dates and signatures remain contradictory; the report can be ordered, but it cannot be made fully consistent.

**Stage C — Investigate the security chair (4–5 min)**

- Return to Reception and inspect the chair, desk, monitor, and door from the room's actual geometry.
- The photograph matches the arrangement almost exactly, except the photograph contains a slight glow on the disconnected screen.
- The chair shows no visible body or blood. The horror comes from the ordinary detail of an object that has been waiting for the person who sat there.

**Stage D — Review the last recording (4–5 min)**

- Replay the audio from Night 4 alongside the incident report.
- Dispatch says, “We found you in the booth.” The guard answers, “Then who's answering?”
- This time the recording continues for a few extra seconds. It includes the guard's chair scraping back, one breath, a switch clicking, and then silence.
- Do not add a clear monster attack or a definitive voice saying “you are dead.” The player should arrive at the conclusion by combining evidence.

**Stage E — Submit the final report (5–6 min)**

- The cause-of-death field offers three documentary classifications: **ELECTRICAL INCIDENT**, **SMOKE INHALATION**, and **UNDETERMINED**.
- The game should permit the player to select one, but the official report remains blank in the saved record. The guard cannot turn uncertain memory into certainty by ticking a box.
- The last page prints itself: **EMPLOYEE FOUND AT 01:13 AM. DOOR UNLOCKED FROM INSIDE. NO INTRUDER FOUND. MONITOR DISCONNECTED. IMAGE STILL LIVE.**

### Clue and text beats

- **Preliminary Incident Report:** Keep the existing report's blank employee name, the unlocked door, disconnected monitor, and incomplete cause-of-death field.
- **Optional: Response Team Note:** “We found no one else in the building. No evidence of forced entry. The desk radio was on. We did not hear it transmit.”
- **Inner thought:** “I kept looking for someone who had come inside. There may have been nobody there. I don't know why that feels worse.”

### End-of-night event

The report finishes printing. The guard's hand is visible at the edge of the page, then disappears from the light as though the light source has moved through it. He looks down. There is no physical injury or graphic body reveal. The clock remains 01:13. The next card is simply **NIGHT 13 — THE LAST FEAR**.

**Progression rule:** All four report fragments, timeline reconstruction, chair inspection, audio review, and final report submission are mandatory. Reading the preliminary report by itself cannot advance the game.

## NIGHT 13 — THE LAST FEAR
**Target:** 25–32 minutes mandatory; 32–40 minutes with optional memories  
**Purpose:** Resolve the character's emotional arc, not the mystery of the universe. The final chapter should feel slower, clearer, and more intimate than the chapters before it.

### Premise

The building is almost silent. The flashlight still works, but the player no longer needs it to see the path ahead. The Watcher does not appear as a pursuer. The guard has discovered enough to suspect that he is dead, but knowing the fact is not the same as accepting the end of his control.

The final task is phrased as the last entry in the shift register: **RETURN TO THE DOOR. DO NOT CHECK THE MONITOR AGAIN.**

### Mandatory exploration route

**Stage A — Retrace the shift (5–6 min)**

- The player must walk a shortened but meaningful route through Reception, the Main Hall, Ward 13, and the Service Connector.
- Each location contains one quiet callback: twelve bed frames; the silent breaker panel; the archive console; the Camera 13 label; the corridor measurement sheet.
- The objects do not produce new exposition. They show how much the meaning of those earlier discoveries has changed.

**Stage B — Turn off the systems of control (4–5 min)**

- At Reception, the player can switch off the monitor, set down the radio, and turn off the flashlight.
- Each action removes a different sound or visual cue that has been present since Night 1.
- The monitor briefly offers Camera 13. The player can select it, but doing so only returns to the image of the guard sitting in the chair. It never reveals a clear threat.
- Progress requires intentionally leaving the monitor off. The game should not punish a player with damage for looking; it simply asks whether they can stop checking.

**Stage C — Revisit the last known route (5–6 min)**

- Follow the marks back toward the security-booth door. The route briefly stretches when the player tries to sprint and becomes steady when the player slows down.
- The player hears the old three knocks. They must cross the corridor without hiding or using CCTV to identify the source.
- The sounds should remain ambiguous: a knock, a pipe settling, a breath, then silence. Do not reveal a face behind the door.

**Stage D — Remember the last minute (4–5 min)**

- At the door, the player finds the final memory fragment: the guard stayed at the monitor during the failure, waiting until he could identify what was outside.
- A final inner thought allows the player to name the fear plainly: “I wanted to know what was there before I opened it. I wanted the door to promise I would be safe.”
- A second line follows only after the player remains still for a moment: “It never promised me anything.”
- The player is not asked to defeat or forgive the Watcher. The figure is absent from the corridor.

**Stage E — Open the door (4–5 min)**

- The player can now interact with the door. There is no lock puzzle. The interaction is not a test of skill, but the culmination of the entire game's behaviour.
- When the player opens it, the room beyond is not a hellscape, a heaven, or a literal afterlife landscape. It is a vast dark space without a visible floor or ceiling.
- The guard's body is not shown. The building dissolves as a set of sounds and fragments: the radio click, fluorescent buzz, the clock, the chair scraping back.
- A distant light appears. It does not explain itself or call the player by name.

### Final decision: “What did I believe?”

The player chooses one of four statements. These are not conventional good/bad endings, and no ending is labelled true, false, reward, or punishment. They are four possible accounts of what a person might experience at the edge of consciousness, shaped by belief and fear.

#### Ending A — “I believed I would be welcomed.”

The dark slowly becomes a soft, indistinct brightness. There may be the impression of a familiar voice, but no face is revealed and no religion is confirmed. The guard feels that he is expected somewhere. The light grows until the screen is white. The final text is: **“Perhaps something was waiting. Perhaps he had finally stopped looking for proof.”**

#### Ending B — “I believed I deserved punishment.”

The brightness never arrives immediately. The dark is filled with sounds of unfinished reports, crossed-out mistakes, and the guard asking what he should have done differently. There is no literal fire, torture chamber, demon, or judge. The experience resembles being trapped in his own certainty that every mistake must be paid for. Eventually the voice of Dispatch says, “You can stop checking the report now.” The sound falls away, but the game does not tell the player what lies beyond it.

#### Ending C — “I never knew what came next.”

The space remains completely dark, but it no longer feels like a corridor. The player has no body, flashlight, chair, or doors to control—only awareness. After a long silence, a very small light appears far away. The player drifts toward it, not because the game confirms it is heaven, but because moving toward the unknown is finally possible. The light overcomes the darkness. Cut to white.

#### Ending D — “I was still afraid.”

The guard does not cross immediately. The door remains open behind him. The darkness is quiet. The player can hear three knocks once more, but they are distant and harmless in the sense that nothing attacks. The last text is: **“He had been afraid. He was still afraid. For the first time, he did not mistake that for failure.”** The screen slowly brightens without showing what is beyond it.

All four endings are canonical as possible subjective experiences. They do not establish a single universal description of death. They are variations on the same emotional resolution: the guard no longer needs to know exactly what waits beyond the door before he can let go.

### Final scene rules

- No explanatory narrator should summarize the metaphysics after the choice.
- Do not show a corpse in a graphic close-up.
- Do not turn the ending into a monster fight.
- Do not describe the afterlife as a scientifically verified place.
- The bright light is an experience, not proof of one belief system.
- Leave several seconds of silence before the final credits. Let the player sit with the image.

**Progression rule:** Night 13 requires completing the retrace route, turning off the control systems, crossing the corridor without hiding or consulting CCTV, reading the final memory, and opening the door. The ending selection follows that sequence; none of the actions before it should auto-trigger credits.

# 8. Progression logic: what the game must enforce

The current automatic transition logic is too permissive. Replace “one key flag + crossing a coordinate” with a stage-based state for each night. It can remain simple data in the existing save object; it does not require a new engine or new assets.

For each night, track:

- `visited`: required rooms or observation points visited.
- `evidence`: required documents, recordings, or fragments recovered.
- `puzzleSolved`: the investigation-specific puzzle completed.
- `eventSeen`: the narrative turning point has actually played.
- `reportFiled`: the player returned to the relevant desk/console and submitted the result.
- `nightComplete`: only becomes true after all required flags are true.

Optional clues should use separate flags. They should never be substituted for a missing mandatory step. If the player crosses a gate too early, keep the chapter active and show a subtle hint about the unfinished task; do not silently advance the night.

### Suggested progression checks

- **Night 1:** Handover read + four inspection stations checked + register discrepancy recorded + camera fault observed + report filed.
- **Night 2:** Maintenance key found + circuit memo read + three breakers set in order + post-repair feed checked + ticket filed.
- **Night 3:** Ward survey complete + all three index slips recovered + archive code solved + ward audit filed.
- **Night 4:** All three reels recovered + timecodes compared + real corridor checked + report synchronized.
- **Night 5:** Signal decoded + Chapel searched + hiding/pursuit event survived + Quiet Room door investigated.
- **Night 6:** Four memory fragments recovered + explanations compared + register entry completed + return report filed.
- **Night 7:** Three measurements captured + changed room inspected + sixth light found + mapless return completed.
- **Night 8:** Camera 13 tested + three image details cross-checked + blind-spot route completed + report filed.
- **Night 9:** Three transcript fragments recovered + sequence assembled + radio tested at three points + radio-off traversal + source classification filed.
- **Night 10:** Three plans compared + three false exits tested + stable path found + handwritten annotation recorded.
- **Night 11:** Three sightings witnessed + three statements collected + gaze sequence completed + Watcher followed at walking pace + transcript read.
- **Night 12:** Four report fragments assembled + timeline ordered + chair inspected + recording replayed + report filed.
- **Night 13:** Retrace route completed + systems switched off + corridor crossed without tools + final memory read + door opened.

### Checkpoint and fairness requirements

- Save progress after every mandatory clue and puzzle stage.
- If the player is interrupted, resumes, or dies during a survival section, do not make them repeat the entire night's document hunt.
- If a required clue is missed because the player can no longer access a room, reopen the route or provide a recovery path. Never softlock the story.
- Avoid forcing the player to read the same long document multiple times. Once read, let the Notes/Case File menu reopen it.
- Puzzles should have in-world clues and a recoverable hint. Do not use arbitrary colour codes without documenting the relationship in a note.
- Never require optional clues for an ending. Optional clues can alter a line of narration or the tone of a final choice, but all four endings remain reachable.

# 9. Low-cost implementation plan for the existing build

This story is designed to work with the current code-first production approach. The priority is progression and narrative staging, not a wholesale model or asset replacement.

1. **Create an authored stage table** for each night. Each stage lists its required flags, entry condition, exit condition, and hint text. The existing HUD objective should describe the current stage rather than the entire night.
2. **Remove transition-on-doorway behaviour.** Doors can unlock mid-night, but they do not end the chapter. Only `nightComplete` triggers the night card.
3. **Re-use the same room geometry.** Different nights change which doors are accessible, which lights work, which props are visible, which CCTV images are available, and which notes are interactable.
4. **Make backtracking meaningful.** A second visit should be prompted by new information, a changed feed, a puzzle result, or a new environmental state—not simply required to inflate runtime.
5. **Use quiet transitions.** A completed report, a clock that refuses to advance, or an image on a dead monitor should often replace a loud jumpscare.
6. **Separate objective guidance from horror prose.** The HUD says what practical thing the guard needs to do. The environment, notes, and inner thoughts suggest what the event means. Do not have the monster give the plot in a speech.
7. **Keep the first six nights physically grounded.** More ambitious corridor distortion and Watcher rules begin in Night 7. The first six can still be frightening through sound, camera discrepancies, missing items, and empty rooms.
8. **Do not hard-code “twenty minutes elapsed.”** Use substantive stage completion, not an arbitrary timer, to ensure the player has explored enough.
9. **Make mobile tasks comfortable.** Prefer a few clear hotspots per room over tiny hidden click targets; make long notes scrollable and persistent; avoid mandatory precision movement during the pursuit sequence.
10. **Test by skipping and replaying stages.** Each night needs a diagnostic snapshot that reports required flags and the remaining conditions. A playtest should be able to confirm exactly why a night has or has not advanced.

# 10. Voice and writing principles

- Keep dialogue rare. The building should not explain itself every few minutes.
- Use short thoughts grounded in an immediate observation. “The room is empty” is stronger than “The fabric of reality is collapsing around me.”
- Make documents bureaucratic and understated. Their ordinary phrasing is part of the horror.
- Let contradictions accumulate over multiple nights. Do not let the first clue reveal that the guard is dead.
- Avoid naming the Watcher as the guard's soul, demon, guilt, or twin. The player can infer those possibilities, but the game does not confirm any one of them.
- Do not equate death with punishment. The “punishment” ending is the subjective experience of someone who believes they deserve it, not a judgement delivered by the universe.
- Do not introduce a final omniscient narrator to explain the story. The report, the route, the door, and the last choice are enough.
- The horror should grow through changes in context. The same chair is ordinary in Night 1, ominous in Night 4, personal in Night 11, and emotionally unavoidable in Night 12.

## The emotional spine in one sentence per night

1. **First Blackout:** “I can still explain this if I check everything.”
2. **Manual Override:** “If I repair it, it will behave normally.”
3. **Ward With Twelve Beds:** “The missing person must be somewhere in the records.”
4. **Original Recordings:** “A camera can prove what happened.”
5. **Unclaimed Door:** “If I survive this, I can return to procedure.”
6. **Quiet Room:** “The evidence is describing a person, not just a building.”
7. **Corridor Measures More:** “The route changes when I try to escape it.”
8. **Camera 13:** “The tool I used to verify reality is showing another version of me.”
9. **Voice on the Radio:** “The voice knows where I am, even when the system cannot.”
10. **Unbuilt Wing:** “I cannot find a stable place by looking harder.”
11. **Watcher:** “It may not be following me; it may be waiting for me.”
12. **Incident Report:** “The person I am investigating is the person who died.”
13. **Last Fear:** “I can open the door without knowing what is beyond it.”

---

## Final design statement

**The 13th Night is not a story about surviving thirteen monster attacks. It is a story about a person who tries to survive uncertainty by turning it into a checklist.** Each night gives that strategy one more chance to work, and one more reason it cannot. By the end, the player has not defeated the unknown. The guard has finally stopped demanding that the unknown prove itself harmless before he is willing to move.
